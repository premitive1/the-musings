Innovation-Regulation Frustration

Commensurate with a career change to yacht maintenance and repair some
three years ago, I familiarized myself with dozens of engineering
systems, about which I knew little or nothing prior. Often times in the
process of my re-education I come across proprietary technology, for
which, in the field, would require permission or license from the
manufacturer to address upgrades or troubleshooting. I have several
times played asssitant to a european trained technician, flewn in from
overseas to oversee a repair nearly identical to what I or my coworkers
would have done anyway. More often, however, I have been unable to
affect a repair due to difficulty in contacting distributors licensed to
sell specialized parts. I work in a very high end, luxury industry, I
shouldn't have problems finding parts. This situation seems extremely
bizarre.

If spending millions, tens of millions, sometimes hundreds of millions
of dollars, I would want something resembling an efficient market for my
business, but the reality is far removed from that expectation.
Gatekeepers, high barriers to entry, and ultimately, the ability to
enforce intellectual property cartels limits competition through
monopoly, leading to significant frustration in the economic and
technical innovation we would otherwise expect in a free market. This
very adequately explains a saying in the trades that cars electronics
are ten years behind consumer electronics, but yachts are twenty years
behind. Don't take me the wrong way regarding how people should conduct
their business. Even without the protections of IP, at any high level of
cash flow, I imagine strong contractual and licensing relationships as
important to any industry relying on insurance policies in case of freak
accidents. However, in a reliance on intellectual property
monopolization to protect profits private firms sacrifice general
innovation borne through the forces not only of competition, but also
applications of novel ideas in non-competing magisteria. Why should cars
and yachts lag behind the standards of other industries? Why can't
companies using parallel technologies likewise evolve in parallel?

IP inventors and investors demand the ability to squeeze every penny
from a good ideas instead of commanding their creativity and capital to
tranform this world into something comparitively utopic. The hardest
part of progess and advancement is not in the having of good ideas. Good
ideas are a dime a dozen. The hard part is in turning ideas into
reality; engineering the reshaping of the world around us to affect the
future of our society and it's on this point where regulation can
horrendously backfire, perpetuating poverty and inaccessibility of
modern civil infrastructure. It impacts quality of life from the highest
levels of finance to the depths of abject poverty. Unfortunately, greed
makes us lazy. Greedy people think it's stupid to compete in a free
market when they can simply build a moat around a revenue stream. Greedy
people think it's their right to build walls around ideas, even through
the corruption of regulatory capture.

The permissionlessness of bitcoin can tear down those walls. The
blockchain does not adhere to unenforceable ideological borders anymore
than humanity adheres to unenforceable national borders. The upending of
the traditional digital landscapes surely beckon a reshaping of digital,
and in turn physical, economies. Even after the hyperbitcoin greed can
still motivate the actions of many, but we have the choice to direct our
attention and our resources to those things which strive towards harmony
with both fellow humans and the natural cycles of the universe. To do
that we need to borrow ideas from each other. We should always be able
to freely use the best ideas humans have had in pursuit of living the
best lives we possibly can. When living your best life, you're spreading
love and wealth. When everyone is living their best life we will all be
rich. Perhaps not rich in money, but I want us to be rich with joy, rich
with culture, rich with happiness. The profits of a life well lived are
a wealth beyond measure because they are your unique experiences. They
are the memories you treasure, and the possibilities you want to share
with your family and friends. And they are worth more than any amount of
money.
