With the timeliness of discussions about immigration into the United
States, it comes up with increasing frequency that many immigrants work
harder than natural born citizens. The reasons are rather apparent even
at a superficial level: where they come from many of these people worked
very hard for very little compensation, but can gain much more pay in
here for much less work. Seen from an other side, Americans get a lot of
money for very little effort compared to most people in the world. We
are so wealthy that we often fail to recognize our own blessings in the
constant self-comparison to people who are somehow even richer than us,
but we should count our blessings. In the journey up the big curve
towards prosperity people born the in the USA dollar economy start off
far ahead of people in discombobulated countries. We not only benefit
from high wages and cheap goods, but also ubiquitous access to some of
the most advanced technology on the planet and therefore regardless of
our sentiments, most of the world has much more to gain from Bitcoin
than the rich. (Though the rich certainly have much to gain as well.)

More prevalent in modern discouse than concern with bitcoin and the
effects of insane monetary policy, many in the *global north* have come
to question the utility or futility of college, and public education
more generally. Though the richest country on earth, too many Americans
got heavily in debt to acquire degrees in worthlessness and find
themselves working careers far removed from their university focus,
regretting ever having agreed to marry a debt scam that can survive a
bankruptcy. Cheap money distorts the values in a community, and
government interference in markets encourages economically unproductive
activities, self-indulgent liberal arts degrees. At some point in the
USA we stopped thinking about what brings value to our family and
society, and got lost thinking we should make money doing anything at
all, without wanting to do anything in particular, without having
dreams, goals, or aspirations. How many fellow citizens can't change a
car tire, let alone the D battery in a household fire alarm? So many
fundamentally important aspects of our standards of living were
indefinitely outsourced for our convenience. It renders us useless and
dependent.

Living in the richest nation on the planet has made us complacent. We
take our circumstances for granted, imbuing our culture with
self-aggrandizing mythologies that white wash our worst behaviors and
the legacies of violence that paved the road to our current prosperity.
For generations this country has socialized the negative externalities
of expansion and industry, while simultaneously protecting the profits
of profiteers--little more than pirates in pantsuits disguising the
criminal nature of society with the veneer of legislation. Inheriting
this violent model of livelihood, the people who run this show have no
incentive to learn and do things outside their comfort zone. For the
rest of humanity, the only way to get ahead in life is to go beyond the
mundane expectations of our lives. The rest of humanity are better
positioned for the new rules of economics that bitcoin will allow,
making it easier to exchange or share value and transform the low-slope
linear crawl of poverty into a big curve towards prosperity. We will
find ourselves surprised by the pace at which formerly poor and hopeless
people advance themselves to the highest echelons of lifestyle and
wellbeing, unrestrained by a delusional fixation on the way things used
to be, instead daring to aspire to how they want the world to be. They
will be the pioneers of the next world.
