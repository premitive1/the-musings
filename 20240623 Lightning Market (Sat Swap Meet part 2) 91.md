The Lightning Market

*This follows up on the conclusion of 20220102 Sat Swap Meet part 1*

When I had the idea of what was then titled the Sat Swap Meet, I had
already been to the big Bitcoin Conference in 2021, where they had a
vendors/art section. I don't mean that my idea was completely novel, but
putting on a bazaar at a much smaller scale would better approximate the
kind of small business idea I alluded to in that earlier essay. Luck
would have it that one of the regulars at Bitcoin Brunch was already
putting on markets in the Miami area, so I spoke with her about the
potential for doing the Sat Swap Meet at brunch with the vendors she
already worked with. While most weeks at Brunch are too small to justify
the effort, whenever a bitcoin or crypto conference would happen in the
area we would usually get an influx of participants. We planned to throw
the first one on the Sunday after the big Bitcoin conference of 2022.

The vendors weren't themselves bitcoiners as such, but as long as they
were willing to accept bitcoin as payment, they could come to brunch
that day and set up a vending table. I suggested that the vendors come
to brunch at least one time before the event so I could try to explain
the idea to them, and walk them through how to accept bitcoin payments,
but unfortunately only one came. On the day of the event, this became an
obvious point of friction, as most of the vendors didn't have a bitcoin
or lightning wallet ready to receive payments, and one seemingly refused
to accept bitcoin payments even though they were told that was the
purpose of the project. Despite these issues, after having walked the
vendors through downloading wallets and how to invoice lightning
payments, things went well. Well enough that we changed the name to the
much catchier Lightning Markets, and held several more coinciding with
the occurrences of bigger, related events. These also went well, though
the friction continued. There was something misguided about a strategy
of hoping to bitcoinize table vendors with the promises of making money
because while they were certainly interested in making money, like most
people outside the confines of Bitcoin Brunch, they don't really care
about what money is and how it works. This creates tension for
bitcoiners who want to support the vendors because we know that their
relationship to bitcoin is fleeting and convenient, while their
commitment to the dollar is indefinite.

Just as with the Sat Swap, the success of the Lightning Markets is more
from their having existed, than necessarily leading to anything more.
With time and continued development of the technology underlying these
practices, I expect such experiments to roll out more easily in the
future than they did for me. Now it seems obvious that ease of use of
Bitcoin is not what will drive its adoption, but Bitcoin being easy to
use will play a pivotal role in the gradual emergence of bitcoin as
money. Today these same projects would prove even more difficult to
execute than two and a half years ago. Many lightning services providers
recently exited the USA in the face of regulatory uncertainty, rendering
many popular bitcoin wallets inaccessible, particularly those that
didn't require their users to identify themselves. Can freedom win out
over the incessant omnipresence of intrusive government regulation?
Large corporate bitcoin services remain viable options for buying,
selling, or trading bitcoin, but the ideal of a free market implied by
bitcoin seems more tenuous today than it did when I first had the idea
for the Sat Swap.
