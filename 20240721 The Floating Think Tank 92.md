The Floating Think Tank

As a result of hosting Bitcoing Brunch for the last three years, many
ideas, technologies, products, and people came to my attention, add atop
that my exposure to bitcoin twitter, and a few podcasts--it's
overwhelming. Part of what overwhelms me is that Bitcoin is a secondary
life to me. I work a full-time FIAT mining job, and have family and
friends from long before who I never persuaded to consider bitcoin as
seriously as myself. I try to imagine what it would be like if I could
at least marry my livelihood with my interest in bitcoin. Seemingly
unrelated to all this, during the pandemic, I made the decision to
pursue a new career in the skilled trades, as a yacht service
technician. Despite growing up in one of the boating capitals of the
world, I had little occasion to spend time on boats, or in the ocean
beyond the beach. Fishing, sailing, and transatlantic crossings were
things I saw in movies, and dreamed about from the shallow sections of
the coast. It wasn't until years later, in the midst of global pandemic
threatening to shape the world into a new normal, I decided I needed
change as well. Familiar with the shortage of skilled labor in the USA,
and influenced by an article in a local paper about the marine services
industry, I decided to learn how to fix boats. In January of 2021, a few
months before I started hosting Bitcoin Brunch, I began a year long
study at the Broward College Marine Engineering Management program. I
knew nothing about mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, or electrical
systems beforehand, but with the help of a good teacher, I quickly
assimilated a vast breadth of information that I have since used for
nearly three years as a professional yacht mechanic and electrician.

As the weeks went on, I began imaging how to bring bitcoin into the mix.
The first and most obvious idea involves my working for bitcoin to fix
peoples boats, but being so new to the industry, I do not yet felt the
confidence necessary to serve as an independent contractor for
Bitcoiners with boats. I also thought of building a shipyard and marina
in El Salvador, and likewise, I'm a little early in this career to
pursue such a thing. Finally I had an idea that seems a little more
practical for my current level of skill and experience, but by no means
a simple undertaking. Bitcoin Boat, the floating think tank, an ongoing
adventure to promote and document the adoption of bitcoin around the
world. Ten to twenty people, among them writers, documentarians,
musicians, and builders, slowly sailing around the world, meeting
people, fulfilling quests, with the overall goal of exposing the world
to the unique manners in which different communities adopt and adapt to
bitcoin as better money. The slow-travel component of this adventure
guarantees the members of Bitcoin Boat stretches of focus time in which
to transform the raw experience and material from their visits into
engaging media to inspire humanity.

Regarding finances, acquiring the boat is the greatest expense. Then,
even if we catch our food and sail everywhere, the maintenance of the
boat represents an ongoing cost. Paint, filters, sacrificial anodes, and
hull repairs are a little of what's required to keep a vessel alfoat. To
alleviate this burden, we can have paying guests subsisize the journey.
Though they may not control the destinations or itineraries, guests
don't have to work, and can just enjoy the ride. And for helping making
it a reality, they will witness first hand what will surely be the
legendary voyages of the Bitcoin Boat on its mission to discover the
next world!
