Vending Machine Standard of Fungibility

In the course of debating the concept of economic utility, and
attempting to convey distinctions between technical and social
fungibility of money, I developed what I consider a very poignant
analogy. The vending machine is a familiar concept to many people around
the world: a container, full of goods, ready to dispense upon receipt of
valid payment. In my childhood there were such machines that would drop
out a handful of colorful spherical chewing gums for a quarter. If you
tried to give it a nickle or a dime, no candy would come out. It only
worked with a quarter. These days electronic sodapop vending machines
are much more common than the simple candy dispensaries of yesteryear.
Their functions remains timeless: if you give it the right money it will
sell you goods. It doesn't care who you are, where you're from, or what
you did before putting in money. It doesn't even care if the money is
stolen, as long as it's the right kind of money!

The example of the indiscriminate ability to use vending machines with
valid currency powerfully highlights what I call technical fungibility.
The physical constraints on the use of an assets. This is in blatant
distinction from the contemporary mores which dominate a civilization,
constricting the exchange of an asset. The social fungibility of a
currency represents all of those rules which are not imposed by the very
nature of a good, but rather by the arbitrary dictates of authorities.
FIAT. If our hypothetical vending machine is located in a store whose
management enforces racial segregation against black people, suddenly
all the money of all the black people in the world becomes non-fungible
in that store, but if a black person has a white friend, and gives that
friend money to go into the store to make a purchase, suddenly this once
tainted money becomes perfectly acceptable again. The problem was never
in the money, but always in the society.

Therefore, when those debates I opened with inevitably come up, I no
longer expend my energy attempting to explain bitcoin's fungibility.
Instead, I turn the spotlight on the naysayer, and ask them: Do you
really want to do business with people who hate you, and work towards
your destruction? Modern society dulls us to the reality that spending
decisions are moral decisions. We constantly do business with strangers,
employees who care little for their work, and less so for the hordes of
annoying, needy consumers. Our economy is heartless. It is part and
parcel with the dehumanization of our species, and the ability for a
money to satisfy the standards of such a system are of little concern to
me. I want to spend my money in a way that reinforces my values within
my community, and I can't do that with dollars. If someone isn't willing
to accept bitcoin, but they're willing to accept dollars, this tells so
much about their values, and their complacent complicity in all of the
oppression and horror manifested by the dollar.
