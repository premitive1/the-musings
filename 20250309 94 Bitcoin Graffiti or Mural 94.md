With the emergence of Ordinals, followed by BRC-20 and more recently
Runes, digital art assets clogged the Bitcoin mempool for about a year
and a half. Despite endless whining on the internet, ideological bitcoin
maximalist hodlers found themselves utterly defenseless against the
onslaught of gambling as distraction from monetary revolution. Scamming
is a necessary tag-along to hyperbitcoinization, but present
circumstances highlight, rather succinctly, the extent to which the
preoccupation with hodling, instead of economically productive activity,
invites the kind of superficial money changing some find so
counterproductive today. Don’t take me as denigrating people who save
their money, as I find it a noble activity, but what are we saving? What
is there to save aside from future consumption? If not our own, then
that of our family, loved ones, and posterity. Sound money represents
the conscious collectivization of social values, a lubricant for
consumptive efficiency, and economic consistency.

I try to imagine a world in which people find Bitcoin so useful they
actually use it for the exchange of goods and services! What if, rather
than full of memes the mempool was full of so-called financial
transactions? If the mempool fills with high paying customers, will
miners sacrifice fees to stick art in blocks? Will cultural patrons pay
top dollar to have their favorite pieces cemented into the blockchain?
Then would digital art asset peddlers have to ask themselves if what
they put on the blockchain is timeless art, or temporary graffiti? Or
putting this sentiment most generally, to what extent do inscriptions
vandalize Bitcoin, and to what extent could someone create a world
wonder on the Blockchain?

Given what we understand as permanence in bitcoin, we should thoroughly
consider the implications of what we leave in the blockchain for
everyone else to see, potentially for the rest of time. In some ways, I
write all of this to acknowledge that there are things more important
than money, but we often acquire them or protect them at great financial
expense. Despite my sympathies with certain paradigms of understanding
bitcoin, regardless of my deeply held preferences, I ultimately succumb
to the will of free market forces. The desperation to exploit the value
of Bitcoin acquires vitality from bitcoins strength. I think any
attempts to fight bad behavior, rather than promote economically
productive behavior, must result in disappointment. The qualities of the
bitcoin blockchain, such as immutability and permissionlessness, so
greatly surpass the scope of our desires, that people will inevitably
seek to build monuments with it. Some of those monuments will fill large
spaces in the physical world, but some will fill tiny, invinsible spaces
in a block, and will live rent free forever in our minds.
