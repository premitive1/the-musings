The Consesus War

*The never ending struggle to define reality.*

\-

The bitcoin network is a new iteration of the social contract. We want
it to embody the best in humanity, and human potential. We want to
wisely address shortcomings in its programming. The unexpected
dissatisfaction many experienced as inscriptions flooded the mempool in
2023 led many to reflect on whether or not they actually like how
bitcoin works. A large swath of bitcoiners had to collectively work
through a technical situation few were intellectually prepared to
understand. Ethical red lines were drawn in the metaphysical sand.
Without fully recognizing the reasons, many found themselves blaming
taproot and segwit for an attack on bitcoin. As a result, many
nontechnical bitcoiners cannot consider how we should upgrade bitcoin
without first getting mired in thoughts of how to establish a new
consensus.

The next war is over how bitcoin evolves, more than what it evolves
into. Yet they are the same war. Some people take their soft fork
proposals for granted as being obvious changes needed for bitcoin to
carry itself into the future. They worry that without their ideas,
bitcoin will not fulfill its adoption potential. This hubris blinds them
to the overwhelming work of getting a mass of individuals to freely move
in the same direction. Organizing a cabal of compliant technocrats isn’t
the whole of the effort. At best it’s a first step. We need more people
to develop for bitcoin and participate in the blood bath that is open
source development. After thoroughly debating and analyzing the idea
themselves, a group of knowledgeable missionaries could then take the
esoteric knowledge and attempt translating it into messaging the rest of
the network can understand. Perhaps if the rest of the network also
likes the idea it might stand a chance to make it into consensus.

As long as the network runs, as long as the cryptography holds, bitcoin
cannot be stopped, but we could stop using it if it becomes impractical
or exorbitantly expensive. I don’t think we need to worry about that any
time soon, either in the case of a soft fork or ossification, but we
should take the potential for unexpected consequences seriously. We
should look upon confident soothsayers with patient skepticism, and ask
if we really need what they’re selling.
