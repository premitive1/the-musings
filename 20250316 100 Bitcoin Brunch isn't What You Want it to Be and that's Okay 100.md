Bitcoin Brunch isn't What You Want it to Be and that's Okay

At the outset of Bitcoin Brunch, I wanted to meet other passioante
people who saw bitcoin as a powerful tool of economic change, but I
didn't know what to expect, especially given my commitment to putting in
as little effort as possible. (Isn't five hours a week enough?) Back
then I didn't want Bitcoin to take over my life, but I did hope I could
finally find a way to fit it into my life. It's free as in freedom
money, an ideal I've believed in for two decades now. The goal of living
on a bitcoin standard continues to inspire me. To achieve that end I
needed to find others open to embracing a bitcoin standard, but I found
that much harder to accomplish than my five hours a week allows. Things
didn't turn out as I imagined.

When I began Brunch the hardcore bitcoiners, or Maxis as I would find
them called, didn't enthusiastically emerge out of the woodwork as I had
hoped. They prefer to dwell in their self-serving virtual safe-spaces,
than connect and build the geographically local Bitcoin community. In
2021, as we approached the zenith of Miami's crypto craze, I took some
kind of stand, made some kind of promise to take part in laying the
foundations of the next world that now seems so inevitable. I would not
sit by idly, waiting for it to happen.

Not yet a bitcoin economy, around Bitcoin Brunch has grown something
approaching a bitcoin community, which dares to look more creatively at
the way we perform economics. We don't need to make friends with
everyone from whom we acquire goods or services, but I won't deny the
utility of having friends, both for personal well-being and economy.
Relationships and reputation are worth more than all the gold you can
carry. I am grateful for the friends and opportunities that have come
and will come as a result of this experience.

Thank you.
