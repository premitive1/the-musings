Bitcoin isn't what you want it to be...and that's okay!

It surprises people to learn I don't particularly like money. I'm a
money-is-the-root-of-all-evil type. Growing up I saw too many people
working endlessly to earn little, while a few seemed to swim in wealth
regardless of how extravagantly they spent. In my childhood dreams of
utopia, money played no role whatsoever, and though my young mind
couldn’t comprehend the reasons, I knew something was wrong with our
money. Where did money come from? Why does it dominate the lives of so
many people around me, often determining whether they are happy or not?

Given the shortcomings of our educational system, it should come as no
surprise that most people assume money comes from government, but we
know that governments didn't invent money, they co-opted it because
money proved itself as an economic and social technology long before
they could ever establish any fiat upon it. Money as a price discovery
mechanism eases tremendous economic friction in providing people an
objective means to compare subjective values. FIAT over money distorts
this fundamental feature, not necessarily making a currency useless, but
surreptitiously volatile, and therefore often self-destructive to the
user.

In a world dominated by printing press patronage and mainstream media
monopolies, a technology empowering humans to measure their own values
will catalyze monumental social change. I write all this to say that I
see something in bitcoin which moves momentously in that direction,
where economics is a tool to increase general human utility, rather than
a specialized weapon wielded by oligarchs to entrench their wealth and
power at the expense of others. Don’t let perfect get in the way of
good, and bitcoin is by no means perfect. If .you can see the good, the
obvious improvement over what we have now, then it doesn’t need to be
perfect, and you don’t need to be the one to change it. Bitcoin isn’t
what you want it to be, and that’s okay.
